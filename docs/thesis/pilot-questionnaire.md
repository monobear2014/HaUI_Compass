# Pilot Questionnaire

Administer the relevant post-task item immediately after T5, T7, T9, and T10, before the interview. Use this response scale for every custom item: 1 Strongly disagree, 2 Disagree, 3 Neither agree nor disagree, 4 Agree, 5 Strongly agree, plus “Prefer not to answer” (reported as missing, not as a midpoint).

## Custom post-task items

| Point | Item |
| --- | --- |
| After T5 | The Next Best Action helped me decide what to work on next. |
| After T5 | The reasons and evidence for the recommendation were clear. |
| After T3/T4 | The weekly plan was useful for organising the fictional workload. |
| After T7/T8 | The reflection step was useful for recording what affected my study work. |
| After T9 | The revised plan was useful after the change in available study time. |
| After T9 | I understood why the plan changed. |
| End | I would trust the system's recommendation only to the extent that I can inspect its reasons and evidence. |
| End | The workflow required a manageable amount of effort. |

The trust item measures conditional, explainability-based trust; it does not assert that the system is always correct. Do not average custom items into a named/validated scale unless a separately justified scoring procedure is defined before collection.

## Overall usability: System Usability Scale (SUS)

Use the official, unmodified 10-item System Usability Scale questionnaire and its standard 5-point agreement response options. Present and score it separately from the custom items. Do not add, remove, reword, reorder, or combine items and still label the result “SUS.” Record each item response and the standard 0–100 SUS score if all required responses are present; otherwise report the score missing and retain available item-level data.

The researcher should obtain the authorised wording/version appropriate to the study language before recruitment and record that version in the study materials. If a translated or adapted version is used, name it as such and do not call it the original English SUS.

## Free-text prompts

1. What, if anything, made the recommendation explanation easier or harder to understand?
2. What, if anything, made the revised plan feel useful or not useful?
3. Is there any information you expected to see but could not find?

## Semi-structured interview guide

Ask neutral follow-ups such as “Can you tell me more?” or “What led you to that view?” Avoid teaching, defending, or suggesting desired answers.

1. What was easiest during the workflow?
2. What was confusing or took more effort than expected?
3. How did you understand why the system selected the next task?
4. How realistic did the weekly plan feel for this fictional workload?
5. What did you expect the replanning step to do after the change?
6. What information was missing, if any?
7. Would you consider using a tool like this for your actual coursework? Why or why not?
8. What would stop you from using it?
